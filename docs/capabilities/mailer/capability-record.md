## 1. Purpose and authority

Mailer offers a replaceable, tenant-isolated **email dispatch and transport** capability through a small, provider-neutral contract. Authorised consumers submit already-approved mail instructions; Mailer validates transport eligibility, routes and attempts sending, records technical attempts/evidence, exposes status and handles controlled retry/reconciliation. A headless mailer has independent value for transactional email even without Notifications or an external integration platform.

**Mailer owns:** dispatch request identity, payload/reference validation and bounded assembly for transport, technical acceptance/attempt state, routing decisions within approved transport policy, provider attempt references, transport receipts and diagnostic classifications, retry/expiry/dead-letter state, delivery-evidence qualification, and its own durable events/outbox if it owns persistent state. Retain sensitive payload content only for bounded necessary periods.

**Mailer does not own:** why or whether a person should be notified (Notifications and source domain); source facts, legal content and approval (domain owner); document/template business meaning (source owner/approved template service); membership, addresses or consent as sources of truth (Identity/Profile/Privacy as applicable); access decisions (Authorisation); identity proofing (Authentication/Verification); legal basis (Legal Compliance/Privacy); scheduling policy (Scheduler/Notifications); event transport (Message Queue); business audit evidence (Audit); mailbox hosting, receiving inbound email, human read/open confirmation, or a general marketing campaign/CRM. Provider SMTP/API credentials stay in host secret management, not ordinary settings, logs or exports.

The governing catalogue names Notifications as a platform capability but does not establish an independent Mailer implementation. The blueprint's previous Booking notes below are preserved. Organisation repository search on 19 November 2026 returned **no matching `nuxt-layers/mailer` implementation repository**; therefore no actual root `CONTRACT.md`, exported signatures, runtime, service levels, CI evidence or implementation readiness is verified. This is an **idealised contract**, not a claim about working code.

## 2. Native services, records and lifecycle

**Proposed minimal services:** accept authorised transport instructions, enforce send policy and permitted sender identity, validate recipients and safe content, assign immutable dispatch/attempt identifiers and idempotency keys, queue durable pending attempts when configured, call an independently replaceable transport adapter, record qualified responses, retry only within bounded policy, reconcile ambiguous outcomes, surface delivery failures and administrative recovery, expire/cancel pending requests where safe, and publish committed technical changes. A basic native mode supports direct SMTP or other host-supplied transport without requiring Notifications, Integrations, Scheduler, MQ, Tracking, UI or marketing providers. Without a configured transport, sending fails explicitly; a purely local/mock sink must never claim actual provider delivery.

**Mailer-owned conceptual records:** `MailDispatch` (tenant, purpose/source reference, authorised sender, recipient envelope and minimised content reference, policy/revision, creation/expiry, dispatch status); `MailAttempt` (provider/adapter, request fingerprint and outcome, timestamps, correlation, retriable/terminal/uncertain classification); `MailTransportEvidence` (provider-authenticated accept/reject/bounce/complaint/delivery-event assertion with provenance and verification); `MailRoutingSelection` (effective approved route and capabilities, provider version); `MailRecoveryCase` (ambiguous or failed attempt, controlled operator action). Raw MIME and recipient PII are not universal durable requirements.

**State model:** instruction rejected before acceptance, or **accepted/queued** → **attempting** → **provider accepted**, **provider rejected**, **retry pending**, **cancelled before attempt**, **expired**, or **outcome unknown/reconciliation required**. Later provider callbacks may qualify a provider-accepted attempt as **delivery reported**, **deferred**, **bounced** or **complaint reported**. Later reports can supersede earlier transport evidence with lineage; they do not rewrite historical attempts. `provider accepted` means only that the provider accepted the message for processing; `delivery reported` is a provider claim, not proof that the intended person received, viewed or understood it. Open/click tracking, if ever offered, requires separate privacy/legal decision and is not a success guarantee.

**Recovery:** network timeout after send may mean provider accepted it. Do not automatically create a fresh send that risks duplicate correspondence; first use a provider-side idempotency key or read-back/reconciliation where available, otherwise expose explicit uncertainty and a policy-governed replay decision. Enforce bounded attempt counts, backoff/jitter, rate/abuse limits, suppression of known invalid recipients, provider throttling and durable deduplication within the configured retention window. There is no honest exactly-once guarantee across remote transports.

## 3. Idealised inbound public contract (proposed)

Each entry defines a **logical input**, not a fixed API or framework DTO. No inbound event grants permission by itself.

| Source and input | Purpose; authority | Necessity; validation and unknown/unavailable behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Notifications or approved domain / host: `SubmitMail` with recipient address or authorised opaque recipient resolution, sender identity, subject, plain text and optional safe HTML, permitted attachments, purpose/source reference, expiry, priority, policy and idempotency identity | Caller/domain owns business notice eligibility, content/facts and recipient intent; Mailer owns transport request decision | Essential per send. Server checks caller/tenant, allowed sender/domain/purpose, recipient syntax, header injection, content type, size, malicious links/attachments and required source approval. Missing authority, unsafe content or contradictory purpose → reject; no implicit public relay |
| Authorisation, caller/assurance resolver, Identity context | Tenant, acting subject/service, resource scope, approved operation, appropriate assurance | Essential secure server context. Denial, missing, stale or ambiguous mandatory authorisation → deny and never send |
| Configuration/host secrets: effective per-tenant sender/routing policy, provider selection, volume/rate, retention/retry settings, approved sender-domain status and credential handles | Configuration owns declared values and version; host protects credentials; Mailer applies transport invariants | Essential effective transport configuration; no valid configured route/secret/domain approval → not ready/reject. Never expose secrets in public result |
| Privacy, Legal Compliance, Notifications or source domain: opt-out/consent, compulsory-service notice eligibility, suppression and statutory send restrictions, processing and retention constraints | Policy owners determine lawful send eligibility; Mailer must implement enforceable gating | Conditional on purpose/jurisdiction; where required and unknown, fail closed. Mailer cannot infer consent from an address |
| Template/rendering owner, Localisation, Clock or host: approved resolved subject/body/assets, locale, temporal formatting and version | Source/template owns message meaning; Mailer handles safe transport representation | Conditional. Unresolved required template/locale/time semantics reject, never silently send unreviewed text |
| Integrations or transport adapter / signed provider callbacks: capability limits, acceptance identifiers, callbacks, bounces, complaints, delivery claims and auth evidence | Provider controls remote outcome; Integrations may own connection credentials and verification; Mailer qualifies transport evidence | Conditional per provider. Signature/replay/tenant mismatch → quarantine/reject; timeout → unknown; conflicting callbacks → reconcile |
| Operator through authorised administrative API: retry/reconcile/cancel pending, suspend route, inspect redacted evidence | Mailer owns technical recovery; authorisation decides who may act | Conditional. Check immutable attempt history, current state, approval, version and idempotency; cancellation after remote acceptance cannot unsend |

**Inputs are distinct:** caller commands, authoritative policy facts, pre-rendered approved content and untrusted provider evidence. An email address does not establish identity, ownership, permission or a legal basis. Bulk recipients and mass marketing are **not** implicitly supported by this transactional transport contract; a future explicit bulk-send policy may reuse it without bypassing controls.

## 5. Commands, queries, events and contract invariants

**Illustrative commands:** `submitMail`, `cancelPendingDispatch`, `retryDispatch`, `reconcileAttempt`, `pauseTransportRoute`, `resumeTransportRoute`, `registerProviderEvidence`, `applySuppression` (only against authorised source evidence). Each command validates tenant, caller, policy/version, state-transition preconditions and idempotency. No one can unsend remote mail. API shape and transport protocol remain implementation choices.

**Queries:** `getDispatch`, `listDispatches`, `getAttemptHistory`, `getTransportReadiness`, `listFailures`, `getProviderEvidence`, and scoped export, with privacy-limited projections, freshness/completeness and unknown fields. Do not turn read queries into resends.

**Events:** only Mailer-owned committed state transitions from its owned outbox. Remote callbacks are verified untrusted claims until reconciled; do not emit `EmailRead` or `PersonNotified` as authoritative Mailer facts. Consumers must tolerate duplicates, replay and out-of-order arrival.

**Required context where applicable:** tenant/group scope, trusted caller and effective access decision, originating owner resource/purpose and revision, approved sender and recipient reference, policy/locale/template revision, request and provider idempotency identity, correlation/trace, provider/attempt/evidence identity and qualified status, creation/attempt/observation/effective time with Clock semantics. **No mandatory universal request object.**

**Invariants:** server-enforced deny-by-default access and resource scope; no open relay; no unauthorised cross-tenant sender impersonation; source authority and Mailer authority separate; only verified provider evidence changes delivery-report state; accepted status never asserts human notification; retries cannot silently duplicate high-risk messages; sensitive data minimised, redacted, encrypted appropriately, securely deleted on expiry subject to lawful hold; record meaningful transitions with immutable historical attribution; no direct private source tables or provider SDK leakage in contracts; versioned schema and compatible evolution; unsupported providers/operations return explicit errors. Storage and owner outbox are capability-owned behind host-composed persistence per accepted ADR-0002/0012, not a shared DB assumption.

## 7. Security, compliance, operations and presentation

**Security:** OWASP ASVS 5 L2 minimum, L3 for security-critical flows; authenticated verified sender domains and DKIM/SPF/DMARC configuration/status, anti-spoofing, allowlisted sender identities, TLS/provider certificate controls, no header injection, HTML sanitisation and safe multipart handling, attachment size/type/security scanning and safe retrieval, defence against SSRF in remote attachments, authorised per-tenant quotas, throttling and abuse/complaint suppression, callback signing/replay protection and restricted egress. SPF/DKIM/DMARC posture improves authenticity, not guaranteed inbox placement. Do not permit arbitrary SMTP host/credential injection from untrusted client requests. Protect unsubscribe/support flows and prevent recipient enumeration through public errors.

**Privacy/legal:** UK GDPR/GDPR where applicable; qualify transactional versus marketing purposes, PECR/ePrivacy and cross-border processors as appropriate. Consent is not the sole lawful basis for every service message and must not be invented. Minimise personal data in bodies, tracking and logs; approved retention and legal holds; data subject access/deletion with lawful exceptions; transfer/processor records and confidential content; avoid invisible open pixels or click tracking by default. Legal Compliance/Privacy must determine relevant jurisdictional duties; this design makes no legal certification claim.

**Reliability:** provider-neutral SMTP/API adapter, optional failover only after duplicate-risk checks, operational bounds, retry budgets, rate-limit awareness, heartbeat/readiness, circuit breaking, deliverability/complaint monitoring and explicit degraded modes. Quota exhaustion or absent provider must be visible; high-risk security notices require an escalation route independent of asserted email delivery.

**GUI:** Headless operation is valid; optional Mailer administrator dashboard is reached through the host's sidebar-to-capability-dashboard route. Authorised tenant admins see route/sender readiness, approved sender identities, volume/quotas, per-purpose queue and failures, redacted dispatch detail and evidence timeline, retry/reconciliation actions with confirmations, suppression and provider health. Platform operators receive separately authorised infrastructure-level diagnostics; ordinary users do **not** receive a generic mailbox UI. Notifications or originating domain owns user preference, message-composition and recipient journeys. Forms separate safe operator, supervisor and administrator functions, with simple plain-language statuses (queued, provider accepted, delivery unconfirmed, bounced). Controls and fields must be server-authorised, not merely hidden client-side. Use public contract projections, WCAG 2.2 AA, responsive accessible interaction, i18n and Theme Manager tokens; no direct private tables. Representative composition host: `nuxt-layers-test/application-test-template`.

## 9. Decision discipline, gaps and design challenge

**Established:** Governing authority hierarchy, public-contract independence and provider neutrality, source-owned state/outbox, server authorisation, privacy, accessibility and acceptance obligations under accepted ADRs and normative standards. Notifications is catalogued platform capability. Booking record explicitly separates business confirmation from mail receipt.

**Agreed from prior related owner decisions (not equivalent to Mailer approval):** server enforcement of permissions, independently owned business authority, provider-neutral cooperation and distinct diagnostic/audit evidence.

**Proposed for explicit owner approval:** **MLR-01** independent headless transactional mail-transport boundary with optional administrator UI; **MLR-02** Notifications owns orchestration/recipient preferences and Mailer owns only transport, permitting authorised direct calls; **MLR-03** durable dispatch/attempt/outcome uncertainty, bounded idempotent retries and no human-receipt guarantee; **MLR-04** provider-neutral SMTP/API and host secret-management ports, optional Integrations; **MLR-05** mandatory server-checked sender/purpose/policy/tenant eligibility, privacy/legal controls and no tracking by default; **MLR-06** redacted operational GUI and versioned technical outbox/public status contract.

**Open architectural choices:** **MLR-07** whether direct non-Notifications callers are universally allowed or restricted by host policy (recommend allow only explicit approved purpose/permission; Notifications remains default coordinator); **MLR-08** whether a generic outbound mail **receiving/reply** workflow warrants a separate capability (recommend out of scope pending independent use case); **MLR-09** which delivery evidence states/retention periods and provider failover policies should be mandatory for different risk classes (recommend tenant/purpose policy within non-negotiable security/retention floors); **MLR-10** whether owned durable queue/state should be a first-class requirement for every production transport composition or an optional direct-send mode (recommend durable for asynchronous/retriable sends, direct-send permitted with explicit reduced guarantees and no fabricated receipt).

**Technical investigations / implementation alignment:** discover actual provider capabilities, SMTP/API adapter signatures, signed sender-domain verification, callback authentication, suppression list authority, exact contract failure codes, credential lifecycle, retention limits, bounce evidence, approved template ownership, host composition size and framework-independent consumer acceptance. No implementation repo/root `CONTRACT.md` found by connected repository search; no build, tests or legal audit conducted.

**Independent architectural challenge:** Mailer must not reown Notifications consent/preferences, become an open-relay primitive, reclassify provider acceptance as guaranteed delivery, conflate commercial and security messages, reown templates/identity, or create a cyclic dependency through Integrations/MQ. Exact provider-level idempotency may be impossible; transparent uncertainty is mandatory. Any domain use of transport results as proof of statutory notification requires separate legal/evidence review. Parallel Logging, Localisation and Legal Compliance reviews are provisional until merged and reconciled.

**Sign-off:** **Not yet owner-approved.** The conceptual proposal is documentation-complete for review; acceptance of MLR items, contractual reconciliation and future implementation/CI acceptance are separate. Pre-merge validation must re-read current governing and blueprint `master`, compare active PRs, links, changed files, status/CI and mergeability, then obtain explicit owner approval before any squash merge.
