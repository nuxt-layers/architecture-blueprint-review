## Purpose, identity and classification

**Established:** Authorisation is the authoritative **access-decision** capability: given a trustworthy actor, requested action, domain-owned resource and verified context, determine whether the operation is permitted and under which applicable conditions. It owns **permission policy semantics, roles and their definitions, role assignments, explicit access grants, their controlled changes, effective decisions and access-review records**. It does not authenticate the actor, issue sessions, create principals or groups, store personal profiles, own protected domain records, or equate a successful sign-in with permission.

**Distinct responsibility:** consistent server-side permission assessment, scope and revocation across every application/domain cannot safely be duplicated in domain UIs, Identity or Authentication. Authorisation warrants a separate Foundation capability, independent of whether it is implemented as a Nuxt Layer, framework-independent core or modular-monolith component. Consumers include protected domain and platform services, IAM Integration, application composition roots, administrators and permitted reviewers. Optional screens are merely views of guarded services.

**Decision function:** `trusted actor + action/permission + authoritative resource descriptor + verified tenant/group context + standing + applicable policy + assurance/recency → allow / deny / unavailable` with a reason category and step-up requirements when appropriate. A decision is **not** a domain business outcome, a consent decision, a legal-compliance verdict or a durable promise that the same permission will remain valid later.

## Authoritative ownership and exclusions

| Authorisation owns | Other owner / prohibition |
|---|---|
| Permission definitions/registry, meaning, risk and compatibility; domain capabilities declare the permissions governing their own operations | A domain remains authoritative for the action's business semantics and resource existence/attributes |
| Tenant-scoped role definitions, built-in role policy, scoped/eligible/time-limited assignments and group access defaults | Identity owns group identity, tree, tenant, membership, standing and owner governance; group hierarchy alone conveys no rights |
| Explicit grants, subject/resource binding, constraints, expiry, delegation limits and revocation | Protected domain owns resource ownership, state and creator provenance; creator identity is not permission |
| Access decision, visible-scope answer, denial reason and obligations for enforcement by the caller | Authentication owns sign-in, authenticators, assurance evidence and session lifetime; only trusted server assertion qualifies |
| Governed access changes, approval decision binding, change state and Authorisation-side access review | Identity owns group governance facts/approval settings; IAM Integration coordinates cross-capability workflows |
| Its own durable records, revisions, retained change provenance and capability-owned outbox events | Audit owns accepted evidential custody and review; Logging technical diagnostics; Message Queue transport |
| Access-policy enforcement and decision-time evaluation | Configuration owns scoped settings/provenance, Privacy privacy policy, Legal Compliance mandatory obligations, not Authorisation's rules |

**Invariants:** deny by default; evaluate on the server; make tenant isolation and resource scope explicit and independent; never infer membership, permission or ownership from hierarchy, creator, email, provider identity, client headers, interface visibility or UI state. An authenticated or personally interested actor has no automatic access. Only trusted, current source facts are eligible. A negative result and an unavailable result are distinct. Permission grants cannot bypass lawful restrictions, domain invariants or obligations enforced by other owners.

## Native services and conceptual model

**Established published behaviour:** the root contract documents `authorise`, `requireAuthorisation`, `authorisationQualifies`, `countAuthorisationQualifying`, `listAuthorisationVisibleGroups`, guarded change administration, role/grant functions, administration queries, self-view, maintenance, export/import, erasure/disposal and a transactional outbox. A request combines a trusted principal assertion, permission and resource descriptor; privileged direct functions are documented as server-only and pre-authorised by the trusted host. It currently supports personal groups, group/ancestor-scoped assignments, explicit grants, eligible activation, risk-dependent authentication requirements and tenant custom roles.

**Proposed minimum independently meaningful service set:**

1. **Permission catalogue:** accept versioned, validated domain permission declarations with documented meaning, risk/effect and backward-compatibility handling; refuse unknown permissions and redefinitions incompatible with the registered meaning.
2. **Policy and role administration:** define effective tenant role profiles, permitted scope, constrained applicability and access defaults; request, authorise, approve, reject, amend or retire policy through attributable governance. Mandatory security floors cannot be reduced by tenant preference.
3. **Assignment and grant lifecycle:** issue, activate where eligible, confirm, suspend/restrict where authorised, expire and revoke bounded roles/delegations; prevent self-escalation, unauthorised regrant, cycles or grant widening beyond the delegator's permitted authority.
4. **Resource decision:** obtain trusted membership/standing, resource ownership and context from their owners; evaluate explicit policy and required authentication assurance; return safe allow/deny/indeterminate categories and obligations. Domain code must enforce the returned decision before accessing its store and recheck where a time-of-check/time-of-use gap matters.
5. **Scoping and discovery:** supply constrained visible scopes for pre-filtering and data minimisation. A scope query is a query-planning aid, not blanket access to every record returned; per-item checks remain required.
6. **Governance and access review:** list effective access and its source, stale/unused/eligible privileges, temporary elevation, grant expiry, review deadlines and proposed remediation; approval must bind to the exact reviewed change and be reassessed at application.
7. **Revocation:** invalidate membership-derived effective rights on Identity departure or suspension, role removal, grant withdrawal or resource transfer. State the maximum tolerated staleness for each risk tier and fail closed when freshness cannot be proven.
8. **Portability and maintenance:** controlled import/export, migration, reconciliation, retention, legal-hold handling, orphan privilege clean-up and idempotent retries; never overwrite a conflicting authority without explicit reconciliation.
9. **Evidence and diagnostics:** emit owned changes and significant decision/denial evidence with safe provenance and correlation, without leaking secrets or excessive personal information; distinguish evidence custody, diagnostic logging and delivery acknowledgement.
10. **Headless administration:** accessible, internationalisable server-enforced actions with optional built-in or host-rendered views for roles, permissions, effective access, grants, requests, approvals, access reviews and denied/step-up outcomes.

**Conceptual objects:** `PermissionDefinition` (owner, stable name, semantics, risk, revision), `RoleDefinition` (scope, permission rules, lifecycle), `RoleAssignment` (principal, group, eligibility, effectivity, expiry, provenance), `Grant` (issuer, grantee, resource, scope, expiry, permitted delegation), `AccessPolicy` (validated scope, version, effective period), `AccessChange` (requester, approvals, digest, status), `AccessReview` (reviewer, results, follow-up), `Decision` (bounded evaluation result, source/policy revision, applicable requirement). These are conceptual information classes, not asserted database tables or exact exported types.

**Lifecycle distinctions:** draft/validated/active/superseded/retired permission or policy; proposed/approved/eligible/active/suspended/expired/revoked role or grant; requested/awaiting-approval/delayed/held/applied/rejected/cancelled/expired change; scheduled/open/completed/overdue/reopened review. Not all states apply to every object. The existing contract specifically publishes `awaiting-approval`, `delayed`, `applied`, `rejected`, `expired`, `cancelled`; additional categories above are proposals, not contract claims. A historical approval never silently reauthorises a subsequently invalid change.

## Contract boundary invariants and verified cross-capability information flows

**Status:** The principles in this section form part of the **approved conceptual direction**. References to named functions, types and ports below describe the published [Authorisation root development contract](https://github.com/nuxt-layers/authorisation/blob/eedfcc6c974f8f15df38b554c394b82164bec4bc/CONTRACT.md), not independently verified implementation or new API requirements. The published root contracts of Identity, Authentication, Configuration, Clock and Profile were consulted for boundary consistency; IAM Integration's root contract retains legacy `nuxt4-layers` names and explicitly says ADR-0012 single-call composition is not yet built, so it is **not** proof of a working modern suite adapter. No root contract was available for Audit, Logging or Message Queue in the organisation at review time: their interactions below are conceptual, not existing consumer integrations.

### Boundary invariants — normative and agreed conceptual direction

1. **Trustworthy context, never client assertion:** permit decisions only with server-verified principal standing, authentication/assurance, tenant, group membership and domain-owned resource facts. The browser may identify a requested resource but cannot assert its true owning group, tenant or permission.
2. **Single authority for effective access:** Authorisation owns roles, assignments, grants and the permission decision. Identity owns groups/memberships, Authentication owns credentials/sessions, and a domain owns the protected resource and its business outcome; no duplicate authoritative record is introduced.
3. **Decision is not business approval:** `allowed` permits an attempt within the evaluated context; the domain independently enforces business invariants, legal restrictions and any required later approvals. A decision is not a reusable bearer token, ownership transfer or promise of perpetual permission.
4. **Tenant and resource isolation:** always constrain and independently validate both. Creator provenance, reporting hierarchy, personal data, membership alone, frontend visibility or caller-supplied group/tenant identifiers never imply access. Group-descendant permissions require explicitly scoped rules.
5. **Safely scoped discovery:** Authorisation's visible-group output narrows search/query scope; each protected record still receives its applicable item-level check. A `groupIds` list is not universal approval to inspect all group content.
6. **Freshness and revocation:** distinguish stale, missing and failed authority sources. Membership departure, suspension, grant/role revocation, resource transfer and policy changes invalidate derived access according to explicit risk-based bounds; sensitive operations fail closed when they cannot verify freshness.
7. **Risk and unmet prerequisites:** distinguish ordinary denial from insufficient authentication assurance/recency or unavailable dependencies. A permitted route needing step-up is **not** an immediate allow. Do not disclose internal policy details to unauthorised clients.
8. **Bounded grants and delegation:** require an independently authorised grantor, verified current resource ownership, scope, applicable expiry, and controlled approval; grants cannot expand beyond authorised policy or survive invalid underlying standing.
9. **Mandatory restrictions take precedence:** Authorisation permission cannot override domain-owned integrity checks, tenant separation, Privacy restrictions or applicable mandatory Legal Compliance conditions. Details of generic deny/permit precedence remain design work (AUTHZ-07).
10. **Evidence versus diagnostics:** Authorisation remains source of its durable change events and significant denial facts. Audit, if integrated, owns evidence acceptance/custody; Logging owns technical diagnostics, not authoritative security history. Outbox relay acknowledgement is not proof of Audit acceptance, and no event transport may silently change the decision.
11. **Explicit integration absence modes:** essential Identity/assurance/resource inputs must be available and trustworthy for the operation; optional Configuration, Audit, Logging, queue, scheduler and external-provider integration never silently confer authority.
12. **No assumed dependency cycle:** contracts can be supplied by the composition root/IAM Integration. A logical producer-consumer relation does not imply a direct package import or permission to access another capability's schema.

### What is **not** an output

Authorisation **does not** supply: authenticated credentials or session tokens; authoritative group membership or tenant creation; identity/profile records; owned business-resource attributes or transactions; legal validity; completed business approvals or workflow outcomes outside its own access-change lifecycle; a general-purpose policy engine for every domain; guaranteed delivery to an Audit store; a directly consumed external provider authority; or unrestricted cross-tenant administrator access.

### Cooperation and dependency classification

- **Essential trusted business inputs:** Identity standing/membership/group and tenant data; Authentication assurance for user-session decisions; the protected caller's verified resource/permission context; Authorisation-owned policy and grants; trusted time. These may be supplied via IAM/host composition, not direct repository imports.
- **Essential current-package infrastructure:** host-supplied Authorisation persistence and lifecycle; the current published Clock package requirement. These are operational dependencies, not a new `persistence` capability.
- **Essential consumers for their own protected workflows, not Authorisation's runtime requirements:** every capability with server-protected actions, including Identity, Authentication administration, Profile, Configuration, Accounting, Asset, Inventory, Billing, Publisher and others. They call the Authorisation contract and retain their records/business checks.
- **Important conditional integrations:** IAM Integration for suite-wide joining/approval/lifecycle, Privacy/Legal Compliance for applicable constraints, Audit for required qualifying evidence, and optionally Configuration, Logging, Observability, Message Queue, Scheduler, Profile-based person-name presentation, UI/Theme Manager and Integrations.
- **No assumed direct dependencies:** no existing root Authorisation contract mandates Audit, Logging, Message Queue, a generic policy engine, or an external identity/authorisation provider. Their absence does not turn a denial into a permit.

### Illustrative domain workflow — inventory adjustment

Inventory owns the stock item, its group/tenant context and permitted business transition; Authentication supplies a trusted principal assertion; Identity provides active standing/membership/group facts; Authorisation evaluates `inventory:adjust` against authoritative resource metadata and effective grants/roles. An allow result permits Inventory to proceed with independent validation and commit **only if its own invariants also pass**. Deny or unavailable prevents the operation. If a stock adjustment is committed, Inventory owns its business outcome and originating event. Authorisation owns only its permission decision and any Authorisation-specific evidence. Audit may preserve independently accepted qualifying evidence; Logging records sanitised diagnostics. Neither transport success nor Authorisation's allow guarantees stock-adjustment success.

### Follow-up alignment boundaries

**Established gaps:** ADR-0012 single-call/50-line composition and shared structural ports; present host-driven Identity-owner/default-role synchronisation; optional best-effort denied-decision sink, unlike the agreed risk-based Audit evidence requirement. **Technical design still required:** define trusted principal/resource resolution, exact non-reusable decision semantics, policy version references, revocation time guarantees, explicit denial/obligation precedence, privileged internal mutation authorisation and audit-sink outage recovery. None justifies granting Authorisation ownership of another capability's records.

## Governance, policy and configuration

**Established:** Identity owns actual group governance and membership; Authentication provides authentication time, method and assurance; Authorisation's contract defines risk levels, secure policy floors, bounded staleness, and approved change workflows. The current contract exposes `provideAuthorisationPolicy` and multiple `provideAuthorisation*` registration calls, with host-supplied catalogue, directory and governance.

**Proposed:** partition settings into (1) immutable platform security invariants; (2) approved tenant/group policy choices within those floors; (3) operational/availability/retention parameters; (4) presentation and user preferences. Configuration may store governed selections under its own public contract, while Authorisation owns validation and enforcement of their access meaning. Complex role/grant objects must not be assumed to fit generic Configuration storage. Effective policy has provenance, version, activation and rollback; its removal or read failure cannot silently produce an allow decision. A changed policy is a security-relevant event and may require re-evaluation of existing assignments.

**Open:** safe authorisation policy-expression extension (for example controlled attribute-based rules in addition to existing roles), evaluator versioning, resource descriptors that change during a transaction, explicit deny/precedence semantics, safe delegation chain limits, prevention of privilege accumulation across groups/tenants, and whether domain constraints remain callbacks enforced by the domain or a narrowly defined published policy extension. Avoid introducing a universal generic policy engine or standalone `policy-map` authority by assumption.

| [Logging](../logging/capability-record.md) | Authorisation emits sanitised operational errors, latency, denial rates and correlation; Logging owns diagnostic sinks, not permission state or evidential custody | **Optional diagnostics** |
| [Observability](../observability/capability-record.md) | Decision health, staleness, failure and latency measures without secrets or unauthorised record contents | **Optional operations** |

| [Legal Compliance](../legal_compliance/capability-record.md), [Privacy](../privacy/capability-record.md) | Applicable restrictions, data minimisation, retention, rights and legal holds; Authorisation enforces access within its remit, does not determine legal applicability | **Conditional mandatory constraints**, not competing permission engines |

| Domain capabilities: [Asset](../asset/capability-record.md), [Accounting](../accounting/capability-record.md), [Publisher](../publisher/capability-record.md), others | Domain owns resource type, ownership, status and protected operation; declares permission intent and enforces Authorisation's decision before data access | **Essential for protected operations**, via contracts and composition; no automatic package dependency |

**Dependency discipline:** logical collaboration is not a mandatory compile-time dependency. Host or IAM composition supplies trusted interfaces and avoids cycles. No use of another capability's private database or private implementation. Independent domain-owned business rules (including payment segregation, publication licensing or legal restrictions) remain enforced by their own owner after permission checks.

## Deep interface review — Authorisation, Audit and Logging (15 November 2026)

**Disposition:** This section develops the approved conceptual direction on risk-based accountability and diagnostics. **Established** means current governing principles or the verified Authorisation `CONTRACT.md`; **Proposed** means a logical interface or guarantee for design, **Open** marks technical details without sufficient evidence. The Audit and Logging conceptual records do not establish production integrations. This investigation does not alter Authorisation implementation, propose a new capability, or waive ADR-0012.

### Responsibility and information contracts

| Interaction | Information crossing the boundary | Originating authority / receiving authority | Contract or evidence status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Authorisation → Audit: permission-policy change | Permission/role definition ID/version, old/new effective references (not entire secret policy), responsible actor, tenant/group scope, change request and approval references, risk, committed outcome, trustworthy source event ID | Authorisation owns policy and change; Audit owns receipt, integrity and evidence review | **Established** Authorisation transactional outbox events for role-defined/changed/deleted, roles-imported, group-access-changed; **Proposed** Audit event mapping, admissible payload, assurance tier, acknowledgement |
| Authorisation → Audit: role and grant lifecycle | Assignment/grant identifiers, subject/resource scope, privilege/risk, grantor/requester, approver evidence reference, effective/expiry/revoke time, committed or denied state | Authorisation owns role/grant state; Audit holds independently qualified evidence | **Established** role-assigned/unassigned/expired/activated, grant-created/revoked, assignment-confirmed; **Proposed** governed evidence schema and completeness verification |
| Authorisation → Audit: privileged-access governance | Request ID and digest, approved/rejected/cancelled/held/applied state, independently verified decider IDs, step-up assertion classification, review-overdue and exceptional escalation references | Identity owns standing/approver eligibility; Authentication owns assurance; Authorisation owns access change; Audit owns accepted evidence | **Established** change-requested/decided/held and review-overdue outbox events; **Proposed** complete actor/evidence binding without credentials or unnecessary personal data |
| Authorisation → Audit: denied/attempted access | Originating operation and trusted caller ID, safe permission/resource class, tenant context, denial category, risk and origin time/correlation, **not** complete request or sensitive resource attributes | Authorisation owns decision fact; Audit owns accepted classification and receipt | **Established limitation:** `provideAuthorisationEventSink` receives `AuthorisationDenialEvent` best-effort; `authorisation.denied` is expressly **not** an outbox event. **Proposed** risk-based durable denial-evidence mechanism for specified events |
| Authorisation → Audit: revocation and restriction | Change or authoritative upstream lifecycle reference, affected rights/scope, requested vs effective revocation time, propagation/freshness status and any unknown gap | Identity/Authentication/domain own their source revocations; Authorisation owns affected access state/effective decisions | **Established** Authorisation role/grant expiry/revocation events; **Proposed** completeness of propagation evidence and critical delay reporting, never independent reinterpretation of Identity state |
| Authorisation → Audit: emergency access | Emergency actor ID, verified assurance classification, permitted action, affected group/identity, reason reference, distinct reviewer/notification and account rotation status, independently qualified step outcomes | ADR-0007 divides emergency identity, Authentication, Identity and Authorisation responsibilities; Audit stores accountable evidence | **Proposed suite-wide composed evidence**, not an Authorisation claim that it alone executes or authorises every emergency lifecycle step |
| Audit → Authorisation: protection of evidence | Audit-owned resource descriptor, data classification, owner/tenant and requested view/export/manage action; trusted request actor | Audit owns evidence/resource attributes and enforces result; Authorisation decides access | **Established normative** server-side authorisation before protected data; **Proposed** named Audit permissions/role scopes in future Audit contract |
| Audit → Authorisation: integrity/coverage status | Qualified Audit receipt, failed/unknown capture, compromised verification or missing-source checkpoint | Audit owns evidence state; Authorisation may receive status for risk-dependent operation prerequisites | **Proposed conditional obligation channel**, never an implicit change to Authorisation roles/permissions or proof of an originating action |
| Authorisation → Logging: technical diagnostics | Sanitised failure code, permission class/risk, non-sensitive operation and correlation, dependency unavailable, stale-directory indicator, latency/retry, backlog/maintenance health; tenant-safe partition tags only when necessary | Authorisation owns interpretation of access failure; Logging owns technical log records, routing and sink lifecycle | **Proposed** structured diagnostic interface; current best-effort denial sink must not be silently relabelled as durable Audit |
| Logging → Authorisation: delivery/health | Log sink unavailable/degraded, backpressure, accepted diagnostic reference when provided | Logging owns transport status only; Authorisation determines what operational fallback is safe | **Proposed** optional diagnostics and explicit absence mode, never positive authorisation evidence |
| Logging/technical source → Audit: selected security control event | Registered typed and source-authenticated anomaly, source action outcome, scope, time/correlation, sensitivity and approved purpose | Original security/technical producer owns claim; Audit owns evidential acceptance/limitations | **Proposed only**, not automatic bulk ingestion of debug logs and never a reconstructed authoritative access decision |

**Separation of concepts:** a permission evaluation can be allowed/denied/unavailable; a business command can be attempted/committed/failed/compensated; an Authorisation event can be durably recorded/pending relay/relayed; Audit can be received/accepted/quarantined/incomplete/verified/unknown; Logging can be emitted/accepted/dropped/unavailable. These are **different states** and must not collapse into a universal success flag. A safe correlation reference joins authorised investigative views but never gives read rights across tenants.

### Capture classification and failure behaviour

| Evidence category | Proposed minimum capture expectation | If Audit or transport is unavailable |
|---|---|---|
| Routine low-risk denial and successful reads | Aggregated diagnostics or policy-selected sampled/minimised evidence; never indiscriminate personal browsing records | Core permission result must remain truthful; missing optional telemetry must be separately observable |
| Denied high-risk or critical actions, suspected privilege abuse and security-control failures | Risk-selected, durable source evidence with authorised producer, categorised outcome and bounded capture backlog | Fail closed or retain explicit pending/uncertain evidence state if a mandatory source-capture guarantee cannot be met; do not manufacture an Audit receipt |
| Committed role/grant/policy changes, elevation and governed approvals | Capability-owned transactional outbox and change/source version, with a traceable evidence acceptance/reconciliation path | Current durable source outbox may allow operation completion under **standard** capture; controlled/critical policy can require stronger confirmation before finality |
| Break-glass use, integrity-control changes and exceptional cross-tenant authority transitions | Strongest applicable configured tier, independent review, alerts and verifiable receipt/coverage status as justified by risk | The emergency exception under ADR-0007 must remain narrowly usable; any operational continuation or deferred evidence must follow an explicit accepted risk treatment, not silently weaken Audit or emergency constraints |

**Established published behaviour:** the Authorisation root contract already writes *changes* to its outbox in the same transaction and relays at least once; it sends *denials* only to a best-effort sink. **Proposed:** incorporate mandatory **selected** denial attempts into a capability-owned durable evidence source or another independently justified trustworthy capture guarantee. Deciding the exact event granularity, cardinality controls, loss tolerance and fail-closed threshold is technical/security design work, not an automatic rule that every denied request blocks until Audit responds. For critical operations, a capture obligation is not satisfied by logging a message, placing an unacknowledged queue item or receiving an HTTP 202 alone.

**Failure modes to specify:** Audit unreachable, malformed event, privacy-restricted field, duplicate/reordered delivery, evidence receipt delayed, incomplete producer sequence, mismatched policy revision, compromised signing/clock, Logging unavailable, logging backpressure and simultaneous outages. Record each separately and reconcile from the owning outbox. Never disable permission enforcement because evidence logging is unavailable; the safety of the *requested operation* follows its approved capture tier. Critical high-volume denial traffic needs throttling and bounded safe degradation without hiding abuse signals.

### Avoiding circular dependencies and recursive accountability

- Authorisation has **no hard synchronous Audit dependency** for ordinary permission decisions; Audit calls Authorisation through a trusted supplied access-decision port before disclosing evidence. The dependency graph does not contain an Authorisation ↔ Audit import cycle.
- A captured Authorisation denial is evidence emitted by Authorisation after its own decision; Audit intake separately authenticates the **producer** using composition-level service trust, not an ordinary Audit evidence-view permission check that would recursively invoke itself.
- Audit guards its own evidence reading, retention configuration, verification and export through Authorisation, while Audit maintains its own bounded **audit-of-audit** records. Those records must not recursively trigger an unbounded new decision/capture loop.
- An Audit acceptance failure cannot retroactively turn an Authorisation `allowed` result into a new Authorisation policy outcome. The **owning operation** must separately enforce any mandatory evidence-before-commit prerequisite and report pending/failure.
- Logging may diagnose failures in either capability but must not be used as a mandatory intermediary for evidence custody. Audit diagnostic failures must not recursively trigger escalating logging/capture operations.
- Domain resource permission checks must occur before protected store query (ADR-0006); privileged Audit read/export additionally requires purpose and disclosure controls from its owner.

### Classification, privacy, access and retention

**Proposed contract envelope fields:** source capability and schema version; immutable source event ID (where produced); risk/event family; actor kind and opaque reference; tenant and owning context; requested action, classified resource type and safe opaque reference; applicable permission/policy revision; authentication assurance category (never a token); occurred and recorded times; explicit attempt/denial/committed/pending outcome; correlation/causation reference; defined sensitivity, retention class and capture tier. All fields must be individually justified. Record **absent/unknown** instead of inventing identity, assurance or chronology.

Do **not** include raw passwords, recovery material, session/bearer tokens, detailed resource bodies, personal names/contact details, arbitrary policy predicates containing private attributes, or sensitive request parameters in routine logs/evidence. An IP address or device fingerprint is personal/sensitive operational information requiring lawful purpose, minimisation, retention and disclosure rules; a general request does not justify recording it forever. Evidence subjects, grant beneficiaries or reviewers do not automatically gain evidence-view rights. Audit export, joins, investigation and cross-tenant correlation must be separately authorised.

Retention/erasure must respect the separate Authorisation change/outbox lifecycle, Logging operational retention and Audit's evidence retention/holds. Subject erasure and membership departure do not automatically erase lawfully retained, minimised accountable evidence; conversely, a generic security rationale does not justify indefinite retention. Technical logs are not automatically under Audit legal holds. Time-to-live, retention policy changes, key loss/compromise, lawful redaction and imported records require owner-specific disposition and provenance.

### Representative conceptual acceptance scenarios

1. A role grant commits once; Authorisation emits a stable source event in its transaction; duplicate/out-of-order MQ delivery is reconciled in Audit without duplicate business changes or falsely claiming exactly-once transport.
2. A critical denied privilege escalation produces the configured durable evidence or an explicit failure/pending result; a Logging success cannot hide missing Audit capture.
3. Ordinary permission decisions proceed without Audit availability unless a formally configured evidence-before-commit obligation applies to the **owning operation**. Audit's protected records remain inaccessible when Authorisation is unavailable.
4. A membership suspension revokes effective privileges; audit evidence identifies source and effective status without treating an Identity event's transport as proof that all caches/sessions have invalidated.
5. Audit evidence read/export, including by the recorded actor, is independently authorised; Audit keeps protected records even when Logging is down.
6. Technical logs are sanitised and tenant-partitioned; raw credentials, session material, policy secret attributes and personal profile fields never leak into logging/evidence envelopes.
7. Simultaneous Authorisation outbox relay, Audit intake and Logging sink failure produce three distinct observable states, followed by idempotent recovery and coverage reconciliation.
8. Emergency access remains constrained by ADR-0007 and records its own accountable action, notification, review and credential rotation statuses without conferring general role management or evidence-reading permission.
9. A policy revision is applied under approved governance; new decisions use its effective version, old records retain provenance, and missing old source evidence remains explicitly unknown.
10. A restored tenant cannot replay obsolete role/grant states or copy evidential rights across tenants; existing Audit history maintains classified source provenance and controlled access.

## External modes, migration and continuity

**Established:** the existing contract is native and server-side, with its own persistence, policy, events and no mandatory third-party policy decision provider. **Proposed:** permit a validated external policy source or specialist rule adapter only behind an explicitly approved, provider-neutral interface. Connection alone does not transfer decision, role or membership authority. For each scope, document source of policy truth, allowed write direction, version/effective time, conflict resolution, outage behaviour, provenance and reversion. A remote decision cannot simply be accepted without verifying caller, scope, resource, freshness and mandatory local constraints. No split-brain authoritative role definitions.

For imports, organisational migration, provider outage, backup restore or policy rollback: compare source and target revisions, quarantine conflicts, avoid silently broadening rights, reconcile memberships and ownership, provide explicit partial/failed/unknown status and fail closed where evidence is insufficient. Maintenance/outbox delivery must tolerate duplicate retries without false business outcomes. Break-glass remains restricted by ADR-0007: passkey-only, own identity, no standing roles, only the two authorised emergency actions, alert/review/rotation; never a general access bypass.

## Security, privacy, accessibility and standards

**Established:** OWASP ASVS 5.0 Level 2 baseline and Level 3 for critical operations; WCAG 2.2 AA where presentation is offered; BCP 47/CLDR/ECMA-402 for localisation; ADR-0003/0005 tenant, standing, group and approval invariants; ADR-0006 server-side pre-query access checks; ADR-0007 emergency controls; ADR-0012 single-call composition. Every data store, derived index and cache must respect tenant isolation, revocation and lawful disposal.

**Proposed:** maintain reason-coded, appropriately minimised accountability for access requests and high-risk denials, assignment/grant mutations, privilege elevations, policy revisions, approvals, emergency events and access reviews; never log bearer secrets, credentials or unnecessary profile data. A technical log is not immutable evidence, and an outbox transport receipt does not prove Audit acceptance. Clarify collection, retention and classification before adopting exhaustive per-request audit: volume, data-subject rights, client privacy and availability risks matter. External guidance worth reviewing at design stage includes NIST SP 800-53 AC and AU control families, NIST SP 800-162 (ABAC), NIST SP 800-63 assurance guidance, OWASP ASVS 5.0 and ISO/IEC 27001 controls. These are reference frameworks, not blanket statutory obligations or automatic certification.

**Presentation needs:** native guarded services plus optional accessible screens for tenant role definitions, group assignments and defaults, effective access and explanations, sharing/delegations, elevation requests, approvals, access review, self-access and safe denial/step-up messages. Every action remains server-authorised; hiding a control never grants/denies permission itself. The detailed page structure belongs to a later design review.

## Conceptual acceptance scenarios

1. **Native:** without external IAM or policy engines, trusted local identity and authentication assertions, native role/grant records and a domain resource yield consistent server-side permit/deny results.
2. **Isolation:** a valid principal, role or group from another tenant never confers cross-tenant data access; a client-supplied tenant or owning group cannot override trusted domain metadata.
3. **No hierarchy magic:** parent-child relationships and creator attribution alone confer no permission; explicitly scoped group-and-descendants assignments apply only when all required membership and policy conditions hold.
4. **Departure:** when membership ends or principal is suspended, derived role, delegated and cached rights terminate within stated bounds; sensitive operations fail closed when authoritative standing is unavailable. Independent valid grants survive only where permitted by the normative rules.
5. **Step-up:** a role holder lacking required phishing-resistant/fresh authentication is not allowed; a verified step-up enables only the particular otherwise-permitted operation.
6. **Governed change:** a user cannot self-assign a privileged role, approve their own high-risk request or reuse an approval after a change digest differs; effective rules are rechecked at application.
7. **Time and review:** eligible elevation ends, grants expire, overdue reviews are visible, and maintenance retries do not regrant removed access or erase review provenance improperly.
8. **Domain truth:** moving ownership of an Asset or Order does not silently leave old access; a domain-owned prohibition can still refuse a request that Authorisation considers permitted.
9. **Service and emergency:** service principals are constrained by their own standing and permissions; break-glass cannot browse data, change roles or bypass approvals, and its narrow actions generate mandatory review.
10. **Audit/Logging:** a permission change emits source-owned durable evidence; transient diagnostic failure cannot fabricate success; denial evidence required by policy has a defined capture and failure/recovery path rather than an optional unnoticed sink.
11. **Outage/restore:** dependency outage, stale Identity data, failed clock, malformed provider assertion or conflicting imported policy yields deny/unavailable; restore/reconciliation never silently broadens access.
12. **User interface:** optional screens can be disabled; host-rendered views still use the same server checks and remain accessible and localisable.

## Approved recommendations, work classifications and unresolved design details

- **AUTHZ-01 — Composition (Established gap):** the current root contract advertises several `provideAuthorisation*` calls plus host-coordinated role synchronisation and maintenance. - **AUTHZ-02 — Shared ports (Established gap):** align public caller/access/clock/events shapes with ADR-0012 while retaining trusted Identity directory/governance, resource descriptors and explicit absence modes; verify a minimal host independently.

- **AUTHZ-04 — Revocation (Open):** specify measurable freshness guarantees for sessions, decisions, derived scope sets, asynchronous projections and Identity event propagation, including fail-closed critical reads.
- **AUTHZ-05 — Policy expressiveness (Open):** decide whether existing permission patterns/conditional role rules suffice, and if/when governed attribute- or relationship-based policy extensions are needed; prevent a universal parallel policy owner.
- **AUTHZ-06 — Authoritative domain facts (Open):** define verifiable resource descriptor, as-of consistency and change-of-ownership handling; avoid a general domain data-reading privilege.
- **AUTHZ-07 — Conflicts and prohibitions (Open):** document precedence for explicit denies, grants, suspensions, domain constraints and mandatory legal restrictions; do not infer automatic grant override.
- **AUTHZ-08 — Access-evidence capture (Open):** reconcile the optional best-effort denial sink with risk-based mandatory Audit events and operational Logging; choose an appropriate availability/retention approach without generating huge indiscriminate request payloads.
- **AUTHZ-09 — Policy configuration (Open):** specify version, precedence, scope, approvals, rollback and whether complex definitions belong in Authorisation rather than generic Configuration.
- **AUTHZ-10 — External authority (Open):** whether to support external policy engines or role federation, and what verified scoped authority transition/reconciliation would entail; none is established by current contract.
- **AUTHZ-11 — Governance/reviews (Open):** precise separation of Identity group approvals, Authorisation role/change approvals, delegated grant capability, independence constraints and periodic certification.
- **AUTHZ-12 — Portable history (Open):** describe subject and tenant export/erasure, retention, legal hold, orphan privileges, import safety and non-reversible external disclosures.
- **AUTHZ-13 — Evidence (Unverified):** published contract documents lifecycle and tests but independently measured integration, threat-model compliance, composition budget and acceptance results have not been verified through the root contract alone.

Ask for owner approval only where an actual policy, governance, scope or consequential trade-off remains; do not offload routine technical investigation. A conceptual record may legitimately contain zero, three or dozens of numbered issues. Numbering begins at 01 by convention, not because a fixed quantity is expected. Later detailed design outcomes must be traceable to these identifiers without recasting settled concepts as unanswered questions.
