## Architectural challenge — the material gaps

**1. What counts as stock?** Inventory cannot mean all material things. A quantity of goods under accountable stock administration is different from a Product definition, Asset in use, customer's consigned possession, financial asset, unowned customer goods or stock supplied by a third-party warehouse. Decide the ledger's exact **stock account scope** and which position is canonical without misrepresenting legal ownership.

**2. Six distinct dimensions must never collapse into `ownerId`:** (a) tenant's digital/administrative authority over the stock account, (b) legal-title assertion with supporting source and dispute/verification state, (c) person/party having physical custody, (d) physical site/bin, (e) allocation/restriction/quality status, and (f) financial recognition entity/book. Source of (a) is Identity/Authorisation; Inventory owns stock-record associations and their history; title may be asserted from trade/contract evidence, not adjudicated by Inventory; physical handlers report custody evidence; Accounting decides (f).

**3. Source-of-truth boundary:** A stock position is an *authoritative derived projection* of committed Inventory movements and allocations, not an independently writable number. Each movement has an accountable stock account, product/stock classification, units, scope, source and revision. Separate **on-hand**, **physically located**, **in-transit**, **usable**, **sellable**, **reserved**, **picked**, **quarantined** and **available-to-promise**. Availability is rule-specific and as-of; physically present consignment stock may not be available to sell; allocated stock is not the same as removed stock. Do not allow a configured "safety buffer" to create more stock or assume title.

**4. Cross-organisation changes:** A stock-account transfer involving two independent accountable parties is a linked, explicit **offer/release → in-transit/escrow-like pending → receiving acceptance** sequence, not an instantaneous cross-tenant database rewrite. It does not independently transfer legal title. Include refusal, lost/damaged shipment, disputed count, partial delivery, return and reconciliation; only each owner may finalise its own context. Inter-warehouse transfer *within one stock account* is different from cross-owner handover.

**5. Production:** Product publishes versioned product/BOM definition. Warehouse may operate physical manufacturing/kit work, or a separately justified Production/Process owner may manage production orders; Inventory must only own **material commitments, consumption, stock class/WIP/finished goods conversion and quantitative provenance**, with input/output conservation plus recorded scrap/yield and permitted units. No invented production-order orchestration service in Inventory.

**6. Financial cost boundary:** Inventory captures quantity, source references and observed acquisition/production/loss/cost-evidence references; Accounting applies recognition framework and approved costing/valuation methods, WIP, COGS, landed-cost allocations and journal. **Do not store a competing authoritative stock book value or silently treat selling price, freight quote, labour estimate or warehouse rental as capitalisable inventory cost.** An optional derived cost layer may exist only as Accounting-owned or Accounting-authorised projection clearly labelled as such. In an integration where external Accounting is primary, this does not authorise Inventory to post a local financial journal.

**7. Provider authority:** Distinguish external **channel availability mirrors** (marketplaces, e-commerce, 3PL), **handling/custody evidence** (warehouse system) and **external authoritative stock ledger**. The default candidate should be *native Inventory authoritative* with connectors as mirrors/evidence; an alternative externally authoritative mode requires explicit per-stock-account selection, admission controls, fencing, cut-over, backfill, reconciliation and failure semantics. Merely installing Integrations or losing a connection never changes master.

**8. Domain-level availability:** Reserving for Order, Booking or marketplace consumes Inventory's canonical quantity commitment. Booking may own a timed reservation for *its own capacity pool*, but cannot independently reserve the same material units. Stock checks exposed for catalogues, search, portals and reporting are read-only snapshots; only an acknowledged, idempotent reservation or issue commits stock.

## Revised conceptual authority and lifecycle proposal

**RINV-01 — Stock identity and accountable scope (proposed).** Inventory owns canonical stock account, stock item/lot/serial tracking reference, quantity-bearing position, movement, reservation and adjustment history for goods designated as stock. Product owns definitions, Asset manages assets in service. A cross-account transfer changes the appropriate ledger states only through evidence and both affected domain authorities.

**RINV-02 — Relationships are independent (proposed).** Track tenant/accountable stock book, claimed title and source, custodian, site/bin, availability restriction, financial book reference and effective-time history independently. Each assertion may be unknown, contested, provisional or verified **as evidence**, without Inventory claiming legal adjudication or financial recognition.

**RINV-03 — Reservations and fungibility (proposed).** Inventory owns serial/lot/pool allocation, hold, renew, release and issue with concurrency and idempotency; guarantee no unauthorised double allocation of the same eligible units. A configured backorder may represent unfulfilled demand but never a fabricated physical balance. Respect expiry/shelf-life, ownership restrictions and quarantine.

**RINV-04 — Production conversions (proposed).** Inventory owns quantitative transformations of stocks and WIP, traceability, yields and scrap, never complete manufacturing work-order decision. Product retains versioned BOMs; Warehouse or separately justified Production coordinates physical execution.

**RINV-05 — Accounting non-duplication (proposed).** Inventory retains cost-source provenance and referential quantity evidence, not authoritative carrying value, method or journal; Accounting issues scoped recognised values and reconciliation reports through public interfaces.

**RINV-06 — External sources (proposed).** Native stock master by default; channel and 3PL integrations are provider-neutral evidence/sync and projections. External-authoritative stock master remains an explicit **optional future mode**, with no automatic takeover and published migration acceptance conditions.

**RINV-07 — Inter-party custody, title and Asset handover (proposed).** A transfer across tenants/stock accounts or into Asset uses correlated, idempotent, versioned, compensable handovers; account for divergent effective times, actual custody, asserted title and final stock retirement/activation independently. No double active sale-stock and Asset classification.

**RINV-08 — Read-only availability and operational UX (proposed).** Stock dashboard presents separately labelled balances and restrictions, as-of revision/time, exceptions and source authority; context-specific actions require authorisation and offer separate operational, compliance, reconciliation and financial views.

### Minimum native behaviour and state transitions

Stock account registration and stock classification → validate product/unit/tenant and provenance → provisional receipt/inspection → accepted movement → eligible or quarantined position → reserve/allocate/pick/release/issue → in-transit/hand-off → counterparty receipt or exception → count/variance correction → return/reclassify/stock-to-Asset conversion → archival with evidence.

Maintain immutable movement history and compensating corrections; a count **does not** rewrite history. Lot/serial uniqueness is scoped and enforced so duplicate supplier identifiers can be disambiguated without double-tracking the *same physical unit*. Conversions preserve source/input-to-output genealogy. Reservation expiry uses trusted Clock time, fencing/version semantics and reconciliation after outages. Regulatory recalls or expiry restrictions prevent releases of ineligible lots, subject to applicable law/policy.

## Idealised public inbound contract — business meaning, not prescribed endpoints

| Source and nature | Facts/request and authority | Required / failure behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Host IAM and policy | Verified tenant/account, subject, assurance, decide(action, resource); approved policies, account mapping | Essential; absent/denied/conflicting trusted authority fails closed; no untrusted browser tenant elevation |
| Stock operator/API | Receive, quarantine, release, transfer, count, adjust, convert, issue and reverse with amount/unit, stock account, reason, source, expected revision, idempotency and evidence | Essential for commanded action; reject inconsistent units, negative quantity, stale revision, forbidden stock status, unknown source or duplicate serial |
| Product | Versioned SKU/variant, unit and conversion, controlled lot/serial requirement, BOM and physical versus virtual bundle | Conditional by physical stock type; stale/unsupported definition stops unsafe new stock operations |
| Order/Booking/Shop | Scoped stock reservation, renewal, commit/release, cancel, return reference and source revision | Conditional; source owner decides its business state, Inventory alone confirms stock; uncertain acknowledgement is not success |
| Warehouse/Logistics/3PL | Receipts, inspection, pick, handoff, delivery, loss, return, bin/custody evidence and referenced external revisions | Conditional; events are evidence, never permission or auto-committed Inventory movement |
| Other stock account/Asset | Source/destination, rights/custody evidence, receipt/reclassification acceptance and provenance | Conditional; non-atomic cross-owner operations remain explicitly pending with compensation/reconciliation |
| Accounting | Recognition and cost-evidence queries, reconciled journal/source references, non-authoritative financial projection access | Optional; finance context never mutates quantity except via a separate authorised stock correction |
| Legal Compliance/Configuration/Clock | Mandatory restriction, retention/hold, allowed thresholds, effective date and reliable time semantics | Required when relevant; missing mandatory policy or uncertain expiry blocks unsafe issue |
| Integrations | External ledger/channel mapping, source version, authoritative mode designation, proof of remote effect, cursor/health | Optional; never promote provisional remote observation into local committed stock without authorised reconciliation |
| Scheduler/Workflow | Execution callback or separate approval outcome for stock operation | Conditional orchestration, does not bypass Inventory command validation |

**Inbound validation:** Identify source capability and authority, accountable stock account, permitted purpose, evidence freshness, units/precision, time, revision, idempotency scope and expected outcome; unknown, stale and contradictory inputs must have explicit refusal or pending states. Receiving event ≠ permission.

## Idealised public outbound contract

| Surface and recipient | Meaning and guarantee |
|---|---|
| Scoped stock description — Product/Order/Shop/Warehouse/Booking | As-of tracked quantity by stock account, product, lot/serial, location/custodian, quality/restriction, held/reserved/in-transit and available-to-promise, revision, source and completeness; snapshot **not a reservation** |
| Movement command outcome — operator/Warehouse/Logistics | Committed/refused/pending/unknown, movement ID, debit/credit source/destination, quantity/unit, effective/recorded time, revision, evidence and stable reason |
| Stock reservation outcome — Order/Booking | Atomic authorised held/renewed/released/consumed or refused/uncertain, reserved quantity/expiry, correlation, idempotency, revision. No Order/Booking confirmation asserted |
| Transfer/handover outcome — peer stock account/Asset | Source released/awaiting receipt/accepted/partly received/disputed/reversed, linked references and compensation state; no implicit title/legal adjudication |
| Reconciliation and quality result — authorised operators/compliance | Count evidence, variance, correction status, lots restricted/recalled/expired, discrepancies and resolution provenance |
| Cost and production quantitative evidence — Accounting | Accepted quantities, source costs, purchase/production/transport references, WIP inputs/outputs/yield/scrap and correction chain; **not** financial valuation |
| Owned domain events — Message Queue/Audit/Reporting/Search | Post-commit stock received/moved/adjusted/held/released/issued/converted/reclassified/reconciled events with tenant, source, revision, timestamps and privacy restrictions. Durable Inventory outbox; at-least-once/replay possible |
| Operational status — host/Integrations/Logging/Observability | Authority mode, sync lag, unresolved receipts, stock ledger health, failed reconciliation, technical diagnostics, controlled disclosure |
| Authorised export/report views — Reporting/authorised people | Read-only, versioned, tenant-filtered movement history and stock positions; Reporting owns reports, not quantities |

**Contract semantics:** Commands validate and fence revisions, return durable committed results only when owner store/outbox commits, and support idempotent replay; failures after unknown response require reconciliation rather than speculative repeated issue. Queries state scope, currency/units where relevant, as-of date, freshness/completeness and provenance, and apply server authorisation. Business events describe committed owner facts, are versioned, may be replayed and never confer an independent command right. No universal request envelope mandated.

## Privacy, security, legal and provider controls

Tenant/accountable-stock-account isolation including delegates, worker tasks, exports, external mapping and serial searches; high-assurance, approved adjustments and write-offs; separation of physical receipt and approval where configured; no cross-tenant stock view inferred from public product listing. Sensitive sourcing/cost and custody history need purpose-scoped reads and minimised personal names. Legal Compliance supplies applicability of controlled goods, recalls, trade/customs, consumer/product safety, privacy, retention and holds; Inventory enforces applicable restrictions, not legal interpretations. Use accepted OWASP ASVS 5 L2 baseline and heightened controls where sensitive; WCAG 2.2 AA, BCP 47/CLDR presentation. IFRS IAS 2 and equivalent accounting standards guide Accounting only where applicable; Inventory should not certify compliance. Evidence integrity, retention, redaction, backups, replay recovery and provider source tracking require acceptance.

**Candidate relevant standards for future contract design (guidance, not universal mandates):** GS1 identifiers including GTIN/GLN/SSCC where applicable; GS1 EPCIS 2.0 for event/provenance exchange; UN/CEFACT or UNECE unit codes; ISO 8000 data quality concepts; ISO 9001 traceability where applicable; ISO 55000 for managed Asset handover; IFRS IAS 2 for Accounting's financial valuation. Verify licensing, standards edition, jurisdiction, compliance scope and tool maturity in implementation research; no library dependency is approved by the conceptual record.

### Accepted manufacturing example and allocation of responsibility

Inventory must track materials and resulting parts **throughout manufacturing, not only at a warehouse doorway**. Example:

1. **Raw store / Warehouse:** A marked billet or billet lot with item ID, quantity/dimensions, material grade, batch and traceability is accepted into available stock. The issue to a manufacturing order transfers its physical position to an authorised staging/work-centre or WIP location and commits the corresponding reservation/consumption. No stock vanishes merely because it left a warehouse.
2. **Turning:** Shop-floor check-in at machine/station records *where* the billet is and the material status (queued, in process, paused, complete, inspection hold, scrap), with order/operation and operator/equipment evidence where authorised. Production/Warehouse owns operation execution, machine programmes, tool settings, timing and quality sign-off; Inventory owns resulting physical stock state, quantity, traceability and verified movements.
3. **Multiple milling operations:** Successive station scans and operation-completion evidence change WIP position/condition and may consume input stock, create intermediate parts or segregate scrap/rework. Keep per-operation genealogy, effective/recorded timestamps, operation identifiers, expected quantities, revision and exceptions. A scan alone is a *claim/evidence*, not necessarily confirmation that an operation was performed correctly.
4. **Finishing / preparation / coating:** Check-in/out supports internal and **external subcontractor** custody/site handovers, quarantine, coating-batch traceability, discrepancies and rework routes. Outsourcing never silently grants the contractor title or changes the Inventory master.
5. **Packing / finished goods storage:** Consume documented packaging materials, finish quality checks and convert approved WIP to finished product/serial or lot; scan receipt into warehouse bin or finished-goods location. Product owns the finished-item definition; Inventory publishes committed manufactured item/lot genealogy, finished quantity and available-to-promise subject to eligibility.
6. **Consumables / tools:** Cutting inserts, abrasive discs, cutting fluid, packaging and other supply stocks have their own item/lot position, issue/return/waste records, replenishment and cost evidence where meaningful. An issued disposable cutter/fluid may be consumed against job or station rather than becoming part of finished product; a reusable controlled cutter, gauge or machine may be managed in Asset with Inventory providing only spare-stock/consumable movements. The Production/Warehouse owner records which job/station used what; Accounting decides expense versus capitalisation where applicable.

### Barcode/scan-first material identity and location

**Conceptual requirement:** Each independently tracked material unit, handling container, WIP batch and finished item/lot must have a **stable machine-readable traceability reference**, using a barcode/2D code label or a linked labelled carrier when marking the workpiece is unsafe/impractical (e.g. oily billet, tiny parts, coating, heat treatment). Physical labels can be replaced/reprinted with an audited identity-link history. The barcode encodes an opaque identifier or appropriate standardised data, **never acts as proof of authority and must not encode secrets or sensitive party details**. Scanning captures identified item/lot/container, stock-account, physical station/bin/site, action, source work order/operation, expected revision, quantity/unit, timestamp, actor/device provenance and an idempotency key. Validate applicable rights and current location/state server-side.

**WIP traceability model:** support distinct **unit**, **lot/batch**, **handling unit/container** and **job/operation** identifiers. A bin or pallet barcode identifies the *carrier*, not automatically every nested part forever; put-in/take-out contents relationships are revisioned. A manufacturing lot may split into sublots, merge only with preserved ingredient/lot genealogy and quality compatibility, lose material as swarf/scrap, and have partial output/rework while the remainder stays in progress. A serialised item may change physical form and product classification while retaining a traceable ancestry. If a process produces indistinguishable mass-produced units, preserve appropriate lot-level genealogy rather than mandating a separate serial sticker for each tiny unit.

**Instantaneous state means the latest *confirmed* authoritative position and its freshness**, not omniscient continuous physical surveillance. Display `last confirmed scan`, current station/site/bin, condition, process stage, quantity, source/version and uncertainty. Planned dispatch/check-out and a device's offline scan are distinct from *confirmed receipt*. Require scan-to-confirm for configured stage transitions, a missing-scan exception/reconciliation workflow and, where justified, dual check-out/check-in evidence. Offline/mobile scanners buffer signed or otherwise attributable idempotent observations with trusted device/clock metadata, but reconciliation and conflict checks occur before committing stock. Clock provides time semantics; host/Identity/Authorisation provide trust and access. Scanner and label hardware integrations remain provider-neutral and optional; manual authorised entries with equivalent evidence are needed for accessibility and recovery.

**Location hierarchy:** owner-approved stock account → sites/warehouses and internal/external areas → receiving/staging/work centres/machines/queues/quality bays/coating/packing → bins/containers as applicable, with effective site/custodian definitions from relevant owners. Work centre and station are public references supplied by Warehouse/Production/Location as appropriate; Inventory owns *last confirmed stock position and status references*, not the master facility directory or machine execution. A physical move may happen without a material transformation; operation completion can change condition without changing location. Independent axes prevent a false choice between "in manufacturing" and "at station 4".

**Contract safety:** stage-transition and stock movement operations require source/target account, permitted location and state, expected revision, positive compatible quantities, idempotency, source work reference, reason/evidence, valid title/use constraints and appropriate quality holds. No blind scan-to-commit or duplicate scans increasing balances. Multi-step handovers can be pending/partially accepted, especially across sites, tenants, external coaters and offline scanners. Physical stock is never both fully at the origin and fully at the destination in the same confirmed stock position; in-transit is explicit. Events reflect committed owner changes, not mere sensor observations.

### Further acceptance examples to retain for implementation

- Billet lot of 20 is issued from raw store; five billet units are checked into turning while fifteen remain staged. Inventory shows exact split location, status and quantity without doubling stock.
- One turned unit moves to each of two milling stages in turn. A missed check-in remains an unresolved in-transit/last-known state; it never becomes a fabricated confirmation.
- A machining operation consumes two inserts and cutting fluid; consumed supply quantities are booked to job/station independently of billet consumption, and returned usable inserts are not written off.
- Milling generates swarf/scrap and one failed component; record accepted yield, scrap, quarantine/rework genealogy and permitted recycling/return movements.
- Finished pieces are batched for external coating. Dispatch records custody transition pending receipt; supplier scan/imported event is not automatic evidence of title transfer.
- 100 parts in an inspected finished lot are packed, packaging consumables are issued, and the resulting finished lot is received into a specific warehouse bin. The finished lot is available only after acceptance and eligibility checks.
- Reprint a damaged label and scan an offline event twice; identity persists, replay is idempotent and stale source revision is reconciled rather than silently moving the lot twice.
- An untrusted scanner or unauthorised operator scans a valid barcode for another tenant's consigned goods; no movement or private details are disclosed.
- Shop-floor screen shows last confirmed location, work centre and operation outcome time; queued, offline, disputed or pending evidence is visually distinct from confirmed.

### Operator adoption: additional conceptual requirement, proposed RINV-13

**Status: design recommendation in response to the owner's explicit priority for ease of use and low training burden; RINV-13 is proposed, not yet independently accepted as a numbered decision.** End-user adoption is a first-class, independently evaluated acceptance characteristic, not an optional cosmetic screen adjustment. Operator simplicity must not bypass Inventory's public authorisation, revision, scan validation, audit, source evidence or quality gates.

**Personas and distinct presentations:** A shop-floor operator receives an intentionally small, role- and station-scoped **task interface** for daily movement, scan/check-in/out, material issue, operation handoff, packing and exception reporting. A supervisor receives job/WIP/location exceptions, safe correction/approval, queue management and handover reconciliation. An inventory administrator configures stock accounts, rules, provider modes, labels, thresholds and traceability policies. Operators do not need to know or navigate accounting journals, stock ownership contracts, event outboxes or implementation terminology. All screens remain optional host-composed presentation over equivalent authorised public/headless contracts.

**Scan-first, task-first workflow:** The host establishes authorised tenant, workstation/site and role; the operational owner supplies currently permitted jobs/stages. Show one clearly labelled main action and the next expected scan (job, material, location, container or consumable). Decode stable machine-readable material/carrier identifiers; retrieve and validate server-side current identity and permissions; auto-populate known safe details. Ask for quantities only when needed and show explicit units and measured-vs-estimated distinction. Make one movement or receipt independently confirmable; avoid a hidden cascade across multiple stock changes. Provide hand-held scanner, touch, keyboard and accessible manual-identifier fallback without assuming a costly device or constant connectivity.

**Feedback and recovery:** Use explicit plain-language outcome labels such as **Recorded**, **Not recorded**, **Waiting to sync**, **Needs review**. Present relevant item/job/station/count and as-of status, confirmation ID and what action is safe next. Do not display a misleading success tone/colour when the authoritative operation is pending/unknown. Differentiate wrong item, wrong station, quantity short, quality hold, stale operation, expired permission and offline scan; explain how to fix or ask for help in place. Use reversible pre-commit steps for harmless errors, but post-commit corrections require the authorised RINV-12 correction flow. High-risk write-offs, inventory adjustments, relabels and authority changes require appropriate supervisor checks; avoid redundant confirmations on ordinary scans.

**Physical and cognitive ergonomics:** Consistent words, icons *with text*, layouts, large touch targets and legible contrast; avoid reliance on colour/sound alone. In loud workshops, confirmation must be visually understandable; audible/vibration cues can supplement it. Consider gloves, wet/dirty screens, reflective light, workstation mounting, handheld reach, barcode damage, left/right-handed use and interruptible workflows. Provide language localisation with familiar site terminology and supported SI/trade-unit display; avoid jargon, ambiguous abbreviations and long instructions. Support a keyboard-only path and assistive technologies under WCAG 2.2 AA; do not treat icons/photographs as a substitute for understandable text.

**Security and scope:** A scanned barcode establishes *which object was indicated*, not *who may operate on it*. Identity/Authorisation supplies verified operator, station/device identity where applicable, delegated scope and permitted action. Configure short-lived workstation sessions and safe shared-terminal reauthentication without uncontrolled persistent privileges. No personally identifying or commercially sensitive details unnecessarily appear on shared screens. Supervisors and admins see progressively richer tools only through independent permission checks.

**Operational usability acceptance — evidence before release:** Conduct real contextual studies and usability sessions with the intended range of operators, including new starters, multilingual staff and people with accessibility needs. Cover receiving, raw billet checkout, station check-in, consuming cutting fluid/inserts, split lots, external coating transfer, packaging, stocktake and mis-scan recovery. Measure task completion unaided, critical error rates, time-on-task, first-use success, support interventions, repeated scans, time to recover an error and training required; compare with agreed baseline and risk-specific acceptance thresholds. Capture usability feedback and iterate before wider deployment. Do not invent numerical service-level or training-time targets without measured field evidence. Distinguish *short training for routine tasks* from required safety, equipment and regulatory training, which Inventory must never bypass.

**Reference methods:** ISO 9241-210:2019 (human-centred interaction design), ISO 6385:2016 (work-system ergonomics), W3C WCAG 2.2 AA (accessibility, understandable errors, predictable interaction and touch accessibility) and established usability heuristics (recognition over recall, prevention and clear status). Align and validate conformance according to scope rather than claiming certification. Specific hardware, PWA/handheld application frameworks, notification modalities and target measurements are implementation decisions.

### Example low-training operator scenario — billet to milling

1. Worker signs into an authorised workstation: screen presents **Current job** and **Scan material** with one primary control.
2. Worker scans billet or container: display identifies **Billet lot B42 — 5 pieces**, current confirmed location and expected **Move to Turning**. No exposed ledger or provider configuration.
3. Worker scans the destination station label and confirms **5 pieces** only if the scanned item, station and job match; an inconsistent scan displays **Wrong station — scan Turning 1** without committing movement.
4. Server returns **Recorded — 5 pieces at Turning 1** after Inventory commit; if offline it returns **Waiting to sync — not yet recorded**, visibly retaining a queued local observation.
5. After accepted turning evidence, workflow presents the next permitted step. Worker scans milling station check-in and issues an insert or cutting fluid quantity using a short prompted action.
6. If one piece is damaged, **Report damaged part** collects quantity and reason with obvious **Request review** if a supervisor is required; it cannot silently reclassify other parts.
7. The workstation shows **Where is my material?** using last-confirmed state and timestamp, not invented continuous real-time tracking. Supervisors can investigate missing scans without asking workers to interpret technical event logs.

**RINV-13 recommendation:** Treat simple, minimal-training, scan-first, error-resistant, accessible operator journeys as an explicit public-contract/presentation acceptance obligation, while keeping UI and workflow logic outside Inventory's authoritative stock ledger. This extends RINV-08 presentation principles but does not change RINV-01–RINV-12 domain ownership.

### One contract, three role-appropriate interfaces

A user may have multiple permissions, but changing interface does not change the underlying entitlement. Use a dedicated operator home screen rather than compressing a desktop administrative grid.

### Two supported Android deployment modes, one app

| Constraint | **A. SIM-enabled phone** | **B. Wi-Fi dedicated handheld** |
|---|---|---|
| Network | Cellular/mobile data or authorised Wi-Fi; factory/remote work | Workplace managed Wi-Fi with authenticated/authorised access to the hosted platform |
| Device restrictions | BYOD/corporate-managed policy depending deployment; least privilege | Corporate dedicated-device/kiosk/lock-task policy, restricted app(s), optional SIM-free phone |

| Shared data and service | Same hosted Inventory contract, tenant isolation, audit, scan interpretation, remote authorisation | Exactly the same; local Wi-Fi never becomes trust or legal-stock-authority boundary |

**Recommended architecture:** one responsive, installable **PWA-capable Android client** as initial cross-device delivery target, with a **replaceable thin Android wrapper/native adapter only if necessary** for dependable camera decode, hardware scanner integration, managed kiosk behaviour, device identity, background sync or provisioned peripheral access. Evaluate representative low-cost devices before selecting the packaging. A browser PWA alone cannot guarantee all manufacturer lock-task, MDM, scanner, camera, offline or background behaviour. Android Enterprise dedicated-device management provides managed kiosk/lock-task configurations and managed Wi-Fi, but any selected management product and licensed components require later due diligence.

**Mobile contracts and ownership:** client obtains a scoped, expiring task/session; authenticates person and, separately where applicable, device/workstation; fetches permitted job, stock identity, last-confirmed location, state, revision and next action; sends barcode observation and requested command with idempotency key, source station, expected revision, quantities/units, effective/recorded times and device provenance; receives committed/refused/pending/unknown with safe retry and human-readable message key. Inventory authorises, validates and commits domain changes to its own server-side durable store/outbox. Production/Warehouse owns job/routing/operation decision and quality owner provides disposition. Device has no local authoritative stock account or permission to write financial books.

**Scanner compatibility:** Camera-based 1D/2D scanning is the base assumption where adequate; accept hardware scan-as-keyboard/HID or platform scanner adapter as optional acceleration. Browser BarcodeDetector availability varies by device/runtime and must be feature-detected with a tested fallback; no nonstandard browser API may be the only path. Codes carry opaque stock/lot/container/location identifiers; barcode possession is not authentication. Provide manual entry/reprint and recoverable poor-light/damaged-code flows. Keep camera permission purpose-limited and ensure site barcode placement/size remains operable on inexpensive cameras.

**Connectivity, security and isolation:** TLS to platform endpoints (including on Wi-Fi), device/workstation enrollment where justified, user-specific authentication with short-lived sessions and safe shift handover, server-verified resource actions, isolated app data, encrypted/short-lived local queued observations where supported, no password/long-term secret in barcode or offline queue. For kiosk handsets, disable unauthorised app switching, screen capture or sharing as risk requires, but retain protected administrator recovery/update pathways, emergency functionality and accessibility. Wi-Fi segmentation and network access control are infrastructure responsibilities; neither an SSID nor device membership grants a stock transaction. Device loss/theft or revocation blocks future commands and supports wipe/session termination through the chosen device-management provider.

**Offline behaviour:** distinguish (1) captured device-side scan observation, (2) queued command intent, (3) authenticated server acceptance, (4) committed authoritative Inventory movement. On reconnect, replay with idempotency and revision fencing, reject stale/revoked/ambiguous operations, reconcile physical movement manually where necessary, and notify supervisor. Never display "Recorded" for a scan held only locally or let two offline devices independently promise the same scarce stock. Where management requires genuine offline *physical work*, record evidence as pending and define supervisor reconciliation; reject unsafe authority-dependent stock releases without connectivity.

**Host and governance:** the client is a **presentation composition** using public Inventory and IAM/Production/Warehouse ports, not a second Inventory capability or an extension with privileged access to private tables. User activation and minimum features can be tenant/configuration driven; the operator view may be embedded in a host app, installed as a PWA or wrapped for managed Android. Avoid mandatory heavy native dependencies until hardware testing demonstrates a need. App updates, long-lived device fleet health and printing/label service are separate infrastructure responsibilities.
